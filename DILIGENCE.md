# KINGAI Global Intelligence — Public Diligence Guide

**Product:** https://intel.kingai.work/  
**Strategic contact:** vip@kingai.work

This guide helps investors, research partners, enterprise buyers and data partners evaluate KINGAI Global Intelligence as a professional intelligence product without requiring disclosure of private production technology.

## Product thesis

The product is not intended to win by publishing one opaque ranking. It aims to create a reusable evidence-linked intelligence layer where comparison, uncertainty, source provenance, media context and recurring professional workflow reinforce one another.

## What to evaluate

### 1. Public usefulness
- Are country comparisons understandable?
- Is uncertainty visible?
- Can users follow methodology and sources?
- Does the product save time before any paid layer exists?

### 2. Professional repeatability
- Do analysts return for repeated country or media research?
- Can monitoring become a recurring workflow?
- Does the product reduce duplicated research effort?

### 3. Commercial conversion
- Is there demand for watchlists, recurring briefs, history, team workflows, data delivery or support?
- Can a professional pilot establish measurable value?
- Can public transparency coexist with paid workflow depth?

### 4. Data quality and trust
- Are missing values handled honestly?
- Are source classes separated?
- Is methodology versioned?
- Are repeated/syndicated reports distinguished from independent evidence?

### 5. Distribution
- Can public pages, methodology, source transparency and useful data generate search, citation, sharing and partner distribution?

### 6. Defensibility
Potential defensibility should be evaluated in:
- longitudinal public snapshots;
- source-normalization discipline;
- methodology trust;
- professional workflow depth;
- historical data;
- partner integrations;
- repeat user behavior;
- reliable delivery.

It should not be reduced to one secret score formula.

## Public metrics worth tracking

Useful metrics may include:
- returning users;
- repeat country comparison;
- professional user retention;
- methodology/source engagement;
- watchlist or scheduled-brief adoption when released;
- data/export/API demand;
- pilot-to-paid conversion;
- partner-sourced usage;
- snapshot freshness and reliability.

Numbers should not be published until verified and approved.

## Commercial diligence questions

1. Which user segment returns most often?
2. What professional task is most painful today?
3. Which paid workflow saves enough time to justify recurring spend?
4. What historical depth creates additional value?
5. Which data can be licensed and redistributed under source terms?
6. How will methodology changes be communicated?
7. Which partnerships improve source coverage or distribution?
8. How does the product control infrastructure and AI costs as coverage grows?
9. What evidence would justify expansion into new intelligence categories?
10. Which features should remain public to preserve trust?

## Key risks

- insufficient source coverage;
- stale data;
- methodology complexity;
- overinterpreting uncertain evidence;
- licensing limits;
- professional users not returning often enough;
- building too many adjacent intelligence categories too early;
- confusing public research support with definitive high-stakes advice.

## Confidential diligence boundary

Appropriate confidential diligence may include verified usage, customer/pilot evidence, financials, unit economics, detailed source/licensing arrangements, architecture/security discussion and contractual materials under authorization.

The public repository does not disclose private collectors, proprietary scoring implementation, credentials, unpublished evidence, private topology, anti-abuse logic or confidential customer/partner information.

## Integrity

No public investor narrative should invent users, revenue, contracts, institutional partnerships, financing, certifications, market leadership or real-time coverage that has not been verified.
