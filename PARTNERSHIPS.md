# KINGAI Global Intelligence — Partnerships

**Product:** https://intel.kingai.work/  
**Strategic contact:** vip@kingai.work

KINGAI Global Intelligence is open to collaboration where a partnership improves evidence quality, distribution, professional usefulness or trustworthy access to international context.

This document describes public commercial directions only. It does not imply that a partnership exists unless separately announced.

## Partnership categories

### Data providers
Potential collaboration around:

- public or licensed datasets;
- historical data;
- country indicators;
- risk/event datasets;
- technical measurements;
- metadata and provenance improvement.

Data partnerships should preserve source attribution, licensing and clear distinctions between public, licensed and proprietary data.

### Media organizations
Potential collaboration around:

- source discovery;
- multilingual media coverage;
- story/context research;
- trend and syndication analysis;
- newsroom research workflows;
- transparent source trails.

The product does not equate media volume with truth and should not convert publisher relationships into hidden scoring preferences.

### Research institutions
Potential collaboration around:

- methodology review;
- country or thematic research;
- public-interest datasets;
- reproducible comparative analysis;
- historical studies;
- academic or policy research workflows.

### International-service firms
Consultancies, international operators, relocation/mobility firms, insurers, logistics or market-entry service providers may use country comparison and monitoring as one layer inside broader professional diligence.

### Enterprise software platforms
Potential integrations may include:

- dashboards;
- risk/strategy software;
- procurement/supplier workflows;
- CRM/operations systems;
- internal research portals;
- workflow automation;
- approved API/data delivery where formally released.

### Developers and data partners
Potential paths include public-data reuse, approved data contracts, future API access, co-developed interfaces and distribution into other research experiences.

### Distribution partners
Potential distribution channels include:

- enterprise research platforms;
- newsletters and briefings;
- media portals;
- professional communities;
- academic/research networks;
- international business networks.

## What a good partnership should improve

A useful partnership should improve at least one of:

- evidence coverage;
- provenance quality;
- freshness;
- geographic/language breadth;
- professional workflow depth;
- trustworthy distribution;
- data usability;
- research quality;
- customer reach.

A partnership should not exist only to place logos on a page.

## Commercial formats

Possible structures can include:

- data licensing;
- research collaboration;
- distribution agreements;
- co-marketing where appropriate;
- enterprise integration;
- managed/custom intelligence;
- joint research reports;
- embedded data experiences;
- strategic investment or co-development.

Exact terms depend on product maturity, source rights, compliance requirements and user value.

## Integrity rules

Partnership materials must not imply:

- institutional endorsement when none exists;
- exclusive data access when it is not exclusive;
- official-government status without authorization;
- platform data ownership that belongs to a source partner;
- scientific validation beyond the actual review performed;
- customer or partner relationships that have not been publicly confirmed.

## Public/private boundary

Partners can evaluate the public product, methodology and source model openly. Proprietary collectors, internal scoring implementation, credentials, unpublished evidence, production topology and private security controls remain confidential unless a scoped confidential review is separately authorized.
