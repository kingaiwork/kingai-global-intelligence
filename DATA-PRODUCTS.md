# KINGAI Global Intelligence — Data Product Strategy

KINGAI Global Intelligence publishes sanitized public intelligence snapshots and a transparent methodology. This document explains how those public foundations can support deeper data products without exposing private collection or proprietary scoring implementation.

## Public data principle

The public product should make enough evidence and methodology visible for users to understand what a published value means, where it came from and where uncertainty remains.

Commercial value should be created through workflow depth, delivery, history, collaboration, reliability and support rather than by making the public methodology artificially opaque.

## Current public data value

Public surfaces can include:
- country and territory metadata;
- published rankings where validation requirements are met;
- evidence coverage;
- sanitized events and assessments;
- media/source context;
- methodology versions;
- public source provenance.

Current live availability is determined by the product and published snapshot, not by this roadmap document.

## Potential professional data products

Only where formally released or contractually supported:

### Historical datasets
Versioned time-series snapshots for research and longitudinal comparison.

### Scheduled delivery
Recurring approved data packages or briefs for organizations that need predictable refresh cycles.

### API access
Stable, documented interfaces for approved data fields and use cases.

### Custom watchlists
Organization-specific country, topic or exposure monitoring.

### Team intelligence feeds
Shared evidence and change summaries for analysts and decision teams.

### Licensed integrations
Approved data delivery into partner applications, research environments or enterprise systems.

### Custom research products
Scoped country, sector, media or market intelligence built from approved public and licensed sources.

## What a data customer should expect

A serious data relationship should define:
- schema and field definitions;
- methodology version;
- refresh expectations;
- missing-data semantics;
- source attribution requirements;
- permitted use;
- support scope;
- change/version policy;
- retention/history terms;
- quality and incident handling.

## Data-quality principles

1. Missing data remains missing rather than being filled with invented certainty.
2. Source breadth is not equivalent to factual certainty.
3. Media volume does not directly become a country score.
4. Methodology changes should be versioned.
5. Public provenance should remain inspectable where licensing permits.
6. High-stakes use requires primary-source verification and appropriate judgment.
