# KINGAI Global Intelligence — FAQ

**Product:** https://intel.kingai.work/  
**Business / research / strategic collaboration:** vip@kingai.work

## What is KINGAI Global Intelligence?

KINGAI Global Intelligence is an evidence-linked public intelligence product for comparing countries, understanding structural conditions and fast-moving risk, exploring multilingual media context, inspecting source provenance and seeing uncertainty rather than hiding it.

## Is it just a country ranking?

No. Ranking is one entry point. The product also includes country profiles, country comparison, methodology, source registry, media intelligence, evidence coverage and published public data snapshots.

## Who is it for?

Useful audiences can include:

- researchers and students;
- analysts;
- journalists and media teams;
- international businesses;
- strategy and risk teams;
- policy/NGO/institutional users;
- developers and data partners;
- informed individuals.

## Does a higher evidence-coverage score mean a country is better?

No. Evidence coverage measures how much public evidence is available. It is not a country-performance score.

## What happens when data is missing?

Missing data remains missing. The product does not invent a number simply to make a table look complete.

## Does news volume directly change a country score?

No. Raw news, videos and public discussion are contextual evidence classes. Source volume is not automatically treated as factual certainty or direct country performance.

## Are repeated articles independent corroboration?

Not necessarily. Syndicated or near-duplicate reporting can increase apparent volume without providing fully independent evidence. The product explicitly treats this as a source-breadth and syndication issue.

## Does the system use the same framework for every country?

The public methodology is designed to apply the same core rules and source-class logic across covered countries. Missing source support can still create different evidence coverage.

## Can I use this for travel or relocation decisions?

It can be one contextual input, but it should not replace current government advisories, local authorities, professional advice or primary-source verification for high-stakes decisions.

## Can a company use it for international expansion?

It can support initial screening and comparative context around countries, risk, governance, media and evidence. A serious market-entry, compliance, legal or security decision still requires deeper domain-specific diligence.

## Is there a free public layer?

The product is designed to maintain a useful transparent public layer. Paid value should come from workflow depth, history, monitoring, collaboration, data delivery, support and integration rather than hiding basic methodology.

## Can developers use the public data?

The repository publishes a public static data contract and source registry. Any downstream use must respect source-specific licensing, attribution and the product's published data rules.

## Is the methodology secret?

The public methodology is intentionally visible. Transparency can improve trust. Proprietary collection systems, internal scoring implementation, unpublished evidence, automation and security controls remain private.

## Why keep some parts private?

A public product should be understandable without exposing implementation that would make the production system easier to attack, copy or manipulate. Public/private boundaries also protect unpublished evidence and partner/customer information.

## Is the product real-time?

Do not assume continuous real-time coverage. Public snapshots and update timestamps should be used to understand freshness. A fast-moving situation should always be checked against current primary sources.

## Does KINGAI claim perfect accuracy?

No. Public intelligence has uncertainty, source limitations and changing conditions. The product is designed to make these limits more visible, not pretend they disappear.

## Does AI decide what is true?

AI can assist with organizing, classifying, summarizing and comparing evidence, but the public product's measured outputs follow published rules and attributable data. AI-generated narrative is not a substitute for evidence.
